## 1. World & Setting Premise

* **Genre / Era:** Low-fantasy, **~1930s-inspired** world.
* **Core World Rule:** **Patterns input, channel, and output magic.** Exact mechanical rules for batik (e.g., RGB values, shape language, geometry) will be defined separately, but this single rule governs craft, architecture, anomalies, and biology alike.
* **Protagonist Context:** *Canting Cloth* is **not** set in Indonesia. The player is a **batik artisan** practicing and preserving their hometown's craft inside a broader, cosmopolitan, multi-cultural world.
* **Design Philosophy *(Reverse: 1999-inspired)*:**
  * **80/20 Grounding:** Roughly 80% real-world 1930s era tailoring, climate logic, and lived-in utility; ~20% fantastical/magical element.
  * **Silhouette & Posture Over Exaggeration:** Communicate archetype through stance, line of action, and garment structure rather than cartoonish body proportions.
  * **Tactile Materiality:** Grounded, touchable textiles (linen, cotton, wool, worn leather) and natural/cinematic color grading so crafted batik sits cohesively in the world.

## 2. The Town — Neutral Mercantile Port (1930s Singapore-Inspired)

* **Primary Reference:** **1930s Straits / Singapore-style maritime trade hub** (Kampong Glam / Arab Street live-work shophouses, shaded *kaki lima* five-foot walkways, tropical Art Deco, and Peranakan ceramic tiles blending European, Chinese, and Malay craft).
* **Why This Setting Works:**
  * Functions as a genuinely **neutral crossroads** where travelers, merchants, and local residents of many cultures and races naturally mix.
  * Historically ties together all represented cultures through real maritime textile history: Straits batik trade, Dutch wax-print industrialization, Japanese resist-dye craft & port presence, and Gold Coast (West African) wax-resist adoption.
* **Visual Cohesion Rules:**
  1. **Shared "Lives Here" Signifiers:** Even when characters come from fundamentally different cultures, they share subtle local similarities showing they live and operate in the same town (e.g., tropical/coastal climate adaptations like rolled sleeves, short/tied hair, breathable fabrics; shared local hardware, mended seams, or harbor tags).
  2. **The Hidden Coherent Core *(Locked)* — The Pattern-Engineered Fracture:**
     * The town was built directly over a **fracture in the ground** where ambient magic runs far denser than anywhere else.
     * Its founders **engineered the entire city as a massive, functional pattern**—streets, shophouse tilework, iron grates, archways, and piers all designed to **harness, catalyze, and stabilize** the fracture's energy.
     * **Why it unites everything:** Because magic is both **denser and stabilized** here, it became the ideal neutral **production & crafting hub** (attracting artisans like the player) *and* a safe harbor where "subtly wrong" humans can live, work, and keep their anomalies stable.
     * **Visual tell:** What looks to outsiders like decorative 1930s Straits/Art Deco trim, tiled facades, brass repairs, and local clasps is actually subtle, repeating **conduit/stabilizer geometry** woven quietly across the town and its residents' gear.

## 3. Character & Costume Direction

### Global Costume Theme: "Day Off" (Quietly Ready)
* Customers visit the workshop on their **day off in town**, not geared up to enter a dungeon that hour.
* Outfits are relaxed, practical 1930s everyday/off-duty wear with **just enough subtle readiness** if an altercation breaks out (e.g., a discreet belt knife, leather wrist-wrap, reinforced boots, or understated utility gear).
* **Gameplay payoff:** Keeping base outfits uncluttered lets the player's finished batik garment act as the piece that transforms them into expedition-ready adventurers.

### Making Batik the Hero
* Silhouettes must stay **clear and readable** with low competing surface pattern on base outfits.
* Balance color contrast intentionally: keep base layers **muted/clean** when the batik is vivid, or allow richer base tones when the batik is muted—relying primarily on **pattern density and clean silhouette zones** so the player's dye and inscription always stand out.

## 4. Vertical Slice Customer Roster

Vertical slice targets **2 customers** (Dutch-ish + Japanese-ish) with a **3rd stretch customer** (Gold Coast / West Africa).

### Species / Fantasy Direction (Unified Pattern Logic)
* **Why Humans Become "Subtly Wrong" (Accidental Patterning):** When an injury, impact, or magical event leaves an **accidental pattern** on a human's body (fracture lines, mirror chips, frost veining), it turns that spot into a permanent living circuit that constantly inputs and outputs magic—making their imperfection their power ("a bug and a feature").
* **Where Fantasy Races / `Beings` Come From (Innate Patterning):** Just like *Reverse: 1999*'s Arcanists and Awakened, other races and non-human beings in this world are **born with natural patterns engraved or grown into their bodies**—giving the setting a unified, non-generic origin for every species and lineage (even while the vertical slice starts grounded with two accidentally-patterned humans).

### Customer Design Notes
* **Same-range human builds, opposite presence:** Because Customer 1 and Customer 2 share realistic, similar-scale proportions (also ideal for UE5 animation/rig reuse), their contrast comes from **stance, negative space, and tailoring**:
  * **Scout *(Dutch-ish Male)*:** Coiled, slightly relaxed/folded posture, soft unpadded shoulders, flowing cloak drape. Day-off outfit TBD.
  * **Guardian — Irie Kiriko *(Japanese-ish Female — Building First)*:**
    * **Name & Epithet:** *Irie Kiriko* ("Mist child of the cove"); epithet ***Kiri*** ("to cut")—an ironic title for a Guardian whose sharpest method is offense, rooted in practical dockside/fishing-knife & heavy-catch blade work rather than noble chivalry.
    * **Background:** Comes from a **normal, loving coastal fisherman family** (no tragic backstory). She stays close with them and regularly goes back home to visit (*Pulang*).
    * **Goals & Personality:** Not chasing glory or high rank—she takes high-paying guardian expeditions in the 1930s production city to **buy her family a modern motorized fishing boat** and eventually bring home **pattern-warded sails/gear** for their foggy cove. Along the way, she loves trading friendly bragging rights with her loud family—hunting down rare regional ingredients/spices, souvenirs, and tall tales to bring back to the dinner table.
    * **Posture & Hair:** Upright, squared shoulders, planted wide stance; **short or tied-up hair** so her **neck stays free** against the damp port climate.
    * **Signature Accessory:** Wears a **fisherman's hat** (great silhouette icon & sun/rain gear; can be slung behind her back indoors).
    * **Casual / Day-Off Look:**
      * **Asymmetric upper body:** **Kimono-styled** top molded for guardian duties—sleeved on the sword-hand side with a practical **leather armguard + soft glove** (showing local 1930s port gear mixing naturally with her homeland silhouette), and **sleeveless** on the frozen opposite side.
      * **Lower body:** **Hakama / kimono-style pants**—reads like a wide, flowing ("flurry") skirt in silhouette and motion, while functioning practically as trousers.
    * **On-Duty / Expedition Look:**
      * Equips full **ō-sode** (shoulder plate) + **kote** (armored sleeve) on the sleeved sword arm.
      * Wears the player's **inscribed batik garment** to enhance/reinforce her frozen sleeveless side without relying on clunky armor there.

## 5. Moodboard & Reference Pointers

* **Town & Architecture:** 1930s Singapore Kampong Glam / Arab Street shophouses, *kaki lima* arcades, Peranakan majolica tiles, 1930s tropical Art Deco ports, wooden cargo vessels alongside steamships.
* **Fashion & Era:** 1930s tropical colonial linen/cotton tailoring (Dutch/European), 1930s *Shōwa* *wayō-secchū* (Japanese-Western fusion) & practical workwear, 1930s Gold Coast textiles (*Dutch Wax*, *Kente*, *Adinkra* geometry).
